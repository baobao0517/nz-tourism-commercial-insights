# Executive Brief | NZ Tourism Market Opportunities

**Scope:** Australia, China and the United States  
**Primary reporting period:** 1 July 2025 – 30 June 2026  
**Evidence:** MBIE International Visitor Survey (IVS), Q2 2026 public microdata; separate Stats NZ / MBIE published arrival context  
**Purpose:** Identify commercially relevant visitor segments and practical hypotheses for tourism businesses — not to claim measured marketing ROI.

## Decision in brief

**Do not use a single, undifferentiated international marketing proposition.** Visitor purpose, first-time versus repeat visitation and expenditure composition vary markedly across these three source markets. A tourism operator can use those differences to design differentiated offerings, then validate them through bookings, gross margin and conversion performance.

## What the evidence shows

| Market | IVS sample | Estimated expenditure | Mean spend per visit | Mean daily spend per visitor | Repeat-visitor share |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Australia | 2,496 | NZ$4.35bn | NZ$3,146 | NZ$405 | 76.1% |
| China | 714 | NZ$1.54bn | NZ$5,449 | NZ$635 | 19.6% |
| United States | 575 | NZ$2.18bn | NZ$6,618 | NZ$584 | 32.8% |

*IVS population-weighted estimates; mean daily spend is the weighted mean of each visitor's total treated spend divided by their length of stay. Estimates are not border-arrival counts, and are not adjusted for inflation.*

### 1. Australia: scale, familiarity and customer diversity

Australia is the largest of the three markets in both survey-estimated total spend and observed border-arrival context. Its visitors are predominantly **repeat visitors (76.1%)**. Its weighted primary-purpose mix is **42.6% holiday** and **37.7% visiting friends and relatives (VFR)**. A notable **93.6% of Australian VFR visitors** are repeat visitors, compared with **60.4% of Australian holiday visitors**.

This provides a plausible explanation for the comparatively low *whole-market* mean spend per visit: a single average combines visitor segments with very different purchasing behaviour. Australian holiday visitors average **NZ$4,469** per visit, versus **NZ$1,781** among VFR visitors. Repeated visits do not *prove* price sensitivity, preference for basics or willingness to pay; those require operator-level testing.

**Commercial test:** Maintain accessible, bookable core accommodation, dining and flexible day experiences; segment Australian acquisition and retention campaigns by travel purpose and first-time/repeat status. Test convenient add-ons for high-value holiday visitors rather than assuming all Australian visitors seek the lowest price.

### 2. China: first-time, holiday, packages and shopping

**80.4%** of Chinese visitors are first-time visitors and **74.5%** have holiday as their main purpose (IVS weighted). Shopping contributes about **16.4%** of estimated spending and package tours about **15.2%**. Nearly **18.9%** of the weighted visitor population report positive tour-package spending, compared with **3.8%** in Australia and **6.4%** in the US.

Importantly, a *tour-package cost is a spending category*, not a count of group tours. Package costs can encompass services otherwise reported under accommodation, travel or activities; the survey's treated expenditure excludes international airfares and non-NZ expenditure.

**Commercial test:** Test easy-to-book bundled visitor experiences, appropriate distribution partnerships and shopping-linked itineraries; assess uptake and profitability rather than infer a national income percentile from trip spending. Packages' internal components are not separable from the current data.

### 3. United States: higher spend per visit and stay-based value

US visitors have the highest mean expenditure per visit (**NZ$6,618**) and longest average stay of the three markets (**17.8 days**, all travel purposes). Their mean daily spend (**NZ$584**) is **below China (NZ$635)**, demonstrating why spend per visit and spend per day answer different questions. US mean per-visitor accommodation (**about NZ$1,783**) and dining (**about NZ$1,262**) are higher than for the other two markets in the all-purpose comparison.

**Commercial test:** Test accommodation-plus-experience bundles and longer-stay offers, while examining nights, product availability, price, and observed bookings. High observed spend is **not** itself evidence of higher *willingness to pay* or a demonstrated price premium.

## Historical position — context, not the main analysis

MBIE's September 2026 release reports **3.67 million visitor arrivals in YE June 2026**, versus **3.89 million in YE June 2019 (94% recovery)**. Its release materials state that Australian and US arrivals both reached approximately **105%** of their equivalent 2019 levels. A comparable exact **Chinese-market 2019 recovery rate has not been independently verified** in this repository and is intentionally omitted from the chart. Do not equate the IVS survey-population totals to these Stats NZ border arrivals.

## What management should measure next

- **Australia:** accommodation/experience booking conversion by holiday versus VFR and first-time versus repeat segments; average gross margin per booking; return-visitor retention.
- **China:** bundle attachment rate, distributor conversion, shopping-linked experience uptake and gross margin after channel commission.
- **United States:** accommodation nights, activity-attachment rate, visitor spend per booking and profitability of longer stays.

**Decision caveat:** This is descriptive, cross-sectional, public-data evidence. There is no campaign cost, acquisition cost, booking conversion or operator margin series. Therefore the recommended actions are **tests and prioritisation hypotheses**, not demonstrated profit or ROI gains.
